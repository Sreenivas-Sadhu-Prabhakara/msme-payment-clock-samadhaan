How the clock works

        

          
Under section 15 of the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act 2006 a buyer must pay a registered micro or small supplier on or before the date agreed in writing, and a period agreed in writing may in no case exceed forty-five days from the day of acceptance or deemed acceptance. With no agreement in writing the period is fifteen days. That last day is the appointed day this tool counts to.

          
Once the appointed day passes, section 16 makes the buyer liable to pay compound interest with monthly rests at three times the Bank Rate notified by the Reserve Bank of India, from the appointed day. Interest for each completed month is added to the balance and itself carries interest; the trailing part month is charged as simple interest on a 365-day year.

          
If the money still does not come, section 18 lets you refer the dispute to the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council, which is what the MSME Samadhaan portal is for. This tool prepares the paperwork; you file it.

          

            Assumptions and figures
            
    
              
  • Appointed day = the date agreed in writing, or acceptance + 15 days with no written agreement, capped at acceptance + 45 days in every case.
    • 
              
  • The appointed day shown is the last day of the statutory period. Section 2(b) defines the appointed day as the day immediately following the expiry of that period, so interest runs from the day after the date shown.
    • 
              
  • Interest = three times the RBI Bank Rate, compounded on each completed month from the appointed day, with the trailing part month simple on a 365-day year.
    • 
              
  • The Bank Rate defaults to 6.50% a year and is yours to override above. It changes — check the current Bank Rate notified by the Reserve Bank of India.
    • 
              
  • Where an invoice is part paid, interest is computed on the principal still outstanding from the appointed day, which understates rather than overstates the claim.
    • 
              
  • An invoice settled after its appointed day still shows the interest that accrued up to the day the money arrived, because paying late does not extinguish the section 16 liability.
    • 
              
  • Only a micro or small enterprise registered on the Udyam portal can rely on these provisions. A medium enterprise cannot.
    • 
            

          

          
This tool computes and prints; it never sends, emails or files anything. Figures are a working estimate for your own books — confirm the current Bank Rate with the Reserve Bank of India and take advice before you rely on a notice or a reference. Buyers should note that under section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act 1961 a sum payable to a micro or small enterprise beyond the section 15 time limit is allowed as a deduction only in the previous year in which it is actually paid.
